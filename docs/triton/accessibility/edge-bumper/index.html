Edge Bumper.

With several screens side by side, the cursor has a long way to travel. Push it against the outer edge, and the Edge Bumper lets you jump it straight to the opposite edge, or open a panel of quick options.

Find it in Settings → Accessibility → Navigation & pointer. It starts on.

What the bumper is

When you push the cursor against the far left or far right edge of your screens, a tall curved sliver appears at that edge, level with the cursor. That sliver is the bumper. It shows that the edge is ready for a jump or for quick options.

The seam between two screens is not an edge. The cursor already crosses it freely, so the bumper never appears there.

Jump or open quick options

What happens next depends on whether the cursor leaves the edge or stays on it:

You doWhat happens
Re-tap. Hit the edge, flick the cursor a little way back, then hit the edge again within 2 seconds.The cursor jumps to the opposite edge, at the same height.
Hold. Hit the edge and keep the cursor against it for about a second.The bumper turns into the quick options panel.
Right-click the bumper.The quick options panel opens at the cursor.

For the re-tap, stay close to the edge: within a tenth of the total width of your screens. On two 1080p screens side by side that is about 384 pixels. Move farther away, or wait longer than 2 seconds, and the jump is cancelled. A single bump far from the edge is almost always an accident, so it never jumps.

Leaving the edge commits you to a jump; staying on it commits you to quick options. One touch of the edge can never do both.

Which edges count

An edge counts when the cursor is against the side of the screen it is on, and no other screen continues farther out on that side at the cursor's height.

  • In a stacked or stair-step layout, a screen's side is an edge wherever nothing continues past it, not only at the very outside of all your screens.
  • A jump lands on the outermost screen of the cursor's row on the other side.
  • A screen you plug in after Triton has started works straight away.

Quick options

The quick options panel is a grid of cards. Click a card to run it; the panel closes. Move the cursor well away from the panel and it closes on its own.

CardWhat it does
ReverseJumps the cursor to the opposite edge, the same as a re-tap. It works out the direction from which half of your screens the cursor is in.

Apps can add their own cards: see registerQuickOption below.

Turning it off or on

  1. Open Settings and choose Accessibility.
  2. Under Navigation & pointer, untick or tick Edge bumper (multi-monitor cursor teleport).

Turn it off if the edge reacts when you don't want it to. The change applies at once, with no restart: turning it off also closes any bumper or quick options panel on screen.

Triton saves the choice in its settings file, settings.properties, under the key edgebumper.enabled.

registerQuickOption

An app can add a card to the quick options panel with EdgeBumperController.registerQuickOption. Call it once, for example when your app starts:

Adding a quick option 
import triton.desktop.EdgeBumperController;

EdgeBumperController.registerQuickOption("hello", "Hello", null,
        side -> System.out.println("Opened from the " + side + " edge"));
ParameterMeaning
idA fixed id for the card. Registering the same id again replaces that card, so calling it twice never adds a second one.
labelThe text under the card's icon.
glyphA QuickGlyph that paints the icon, or null for a plain dot.
actionRuns on the Swing event thread when the card is chosen. It gets the edge the panel was opened from: Side.LEFT or Side.RIGHT.

You can call it from any thread, and before the bumper is installed.
