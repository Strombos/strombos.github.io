Gesture Control.

Gesture control lets you work Triton with your hands through the camera: pinch for volume and zoom, twist to rotate, grab to drag. The Accessibility switch decides whether it is allowed at all.

Find it in Settings → Accessibility → Gesture control. It starts on: allowed, but not running until you ask for it.

What gesture control is

Gesture control watches your hand through the camera and turns what it does into actions:

GestureDoes
PinchChanges the volume, or zooms.
TwistRotates.
GrabDrags.

The camera is only on while gesture control is running, never just because it is allowed.

Starting it with Troy

Gesture control starts and stops when you ask Troy, Triton's voice assistant:

SayWhat happens
“Troy, activate gesture control”Starts gesture control for all of Triton.
“Troy, gesture control for app”Starts it for one app, for example “gesture control for neural map”.
“zoom”While it runs, listens only for that gesture.
“unlock” or “general”Listens for every gesture again.
“Troy, deactivate gesture control”Stops it and turns the camera off.

The first start can take a little longer while Triton sets up hand tracking.

The Accessibility switch

  1. Open Settings and choose Accessibility.
  2. Under Gesture control, untick or tick Gesture control (camera hand tracking).

While it is off:

  • Nothing can start gesture control, not even Troy. Asking Troy gets the answer that it is turned off in Accessibility settings.
  • If gesture control is running when you turn it off, it stops and the camera is released straight away.

Where it is saved

The switch is saved in gesture.properties, next to the gesture models, under the key gesture.enabled. It travels with a portable copy of Triton.
